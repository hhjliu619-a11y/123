Figure 1. Evidence selection and analysis workflow. Searches were restricted by study start date, not result direction. The 114 requested record responses represent 113 canonical identifiers because one historical ASPECT identifier now resolves to an API alias. Exact joint analysis includes five programs and six contrasts, with one shared comparator.

Figure 2. World distribution of registered site listings. The map covers 17 eligible phase 3 programs and the APEKS phase 2 extension; 16 programs have posted location lists, comprising 957 listings across 39 countries. Listings were deduplicated within pooled programs, not across programs. Gray means no listed site in this evidence set. Values are neither enrolled-patient totals nor disease prevalence. Made with Natural Earth.

Figure 3. Start years and outcome-specific analysis denominators for the detailed endpoint cohorts. Bubble area reflects the sum of the displayed experimental and comparator denominators. The two INTEGRAL-1 contrasts reuse the same 105-person control and are not additive. The five exact primary programs started during 2016–2023; the search window was 2006–2025.

Figure 4. Compatibility audit for the detailed endpoint cohorts. Unresolved means that a source definition or reported population does not support valid joint reconstruction. APEKS numerators are uniquely inferred from rounded percentages. ALLIUM exact counts come from an FDA snapshot. Neither extension is part of the strict registry primary analysis.

Figure 5. Composite treatment effects in all 13 eligible phase 3 programs with posted results, shown as 14 contrasts. Unfavorable and inconclusive eravacycline and sulopenem results remain visible. A dagger denotes an event count uniquely inferred from a rounded percentage. Original populations and post-treatment windows differ, including the posted IGNITE-2 PT visit; estimates are not pooled or used to rank therapies. Newcombe 95% intervals are unadjusted; they do not replace the original trial noninferiority analyses.

Figure 6. Within-trial crude absolute treatment effects for composite, clinical and microbiological responses, with Newcombe score-based 95% confidence intervals. Columns retain their original definitions and are not pooled or ranked across drugs. An asterisk denotes a source-audit cohort: its component and composite outcomes cannot be treated as a verified joint distribution. A dagger denotes the derived-count phase 2 extension; a double dagger denotes the FDA extension. FEP/NAC, cefepime/nacubactam; ATM/NAC, aztreonam/nacubactam.

Figure 7. Difference between the microbiological and clinical experimental-minus-comparator risk differences, in percentage points. Confidence intervals use the within-arm covariance implied by verified joint counts. Teal denotes exact primary registry evidence; gray denotes separately designated extensions. The two INTEGRAL-1 estimates have a shared control. Their covariance is preserved by combining active arms for program-level synthesis.

Figure 8. Reconstructed aggregate joint endpoint classifications at test of cure in the exact primary evidence set. The four categories are both responses, clinical response only, microbiological response only, and neither documented response. Non-success can include failure or indeterminate assessment and must not be equated with biological persistence. The shared INTEGRAL-1 control appears once. Exact counts are supplied in Supplementary Table S1.

Figure 9. Exploratory program-level sensitivity analyses. Random-effects restricted maximum likelihood estimates use modified Hartung–Knapp intervals. The nacubactam experimental groups are combined to retain one control per independent program. The strict five-program result is inconclusive; the result after adding both extensions is a sensitivity finding and is not substituted for the primary estimate.

Figure 10. ALLIUM source and estimator reconciliation. Crude count-derived risk differences are compared with the FDA-reported stratified Newcombe differences. These estimates use the same public efficacy counts but different estimators; their difference is not evidence of transcription error. FDA source verification does not constitute independent clinical validation.

Figure 11. Participants with serious adverse events in the posted safety populations of the detailed registry cohorts. Denominators and surveillance windows differ from efficacy populations and from each other. Bars are descriptive and do not constitute a pooled safety ranking. PIP/TAZ, piperacillin/tazobactam; FEP/TAN, cefepime/taniborbactam; FEP/ZID, cefepime/zidebactam.
